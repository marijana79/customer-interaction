Customer Interview Types

A practical guide for choosing the right type of customer conversation, depending on the decision we need to make.

As designers, we speak with customers and users for different reasons throughout the product lifecycle. These conversations help us understand real workflows, validate assumptions, identify usability issues, explore future opportunities, and make better product decisions.

The term customer interview is often used broadly, but not every customer conversation has the same purpose. Each type has a different goal, timing, format, and expected output. This guide helps designers, product teams, and stakeholders understand when to use each interview type and why it matters.

Discovery

Customer / User Discovery Interview

Understand users, their goals, needs, pain points, behaviours, and context before defining a solution.

Purpose
To understand users, their goals, needs, pain points, behaviours, and context before defining a solution.
When we use it
Early in discovery, when we are exploring a problem area, new product opportunity, or unclear user need.
What we want to learn
Who the users are, what they are trying to achieve, what challenges they face, what workarounds they use, and what success looks like for them.
Typical questions
  • How do you currently handle this process?
  • What are the most difficult parts of this task?
  • What happens when something goes wrong?
  • What tools or systems do you use today?
  • What would make this process easier?
Output
User needs, pain points, themes, opportunity areas, assumptions to validate, and early design recommendations.
Why it matters
It helps the team understand the real customer problem before jumping into solution design.
Workflow

Current Process / Workflow Interview

Understand how customers currently complete a specific process from start to finish.

Purpose
To understand how customers currently complete a specific process from start to finish.
When we use it
When we need to map an existing workflow, understand operational behaviour, or redesign part of a journey.
What we want to learn
Steps in the process, roles involved, tools used, decision points, manual workarounds, dependencies, exceptions, and pain points.
Typical questions
  • Can you walk us through how you do this today?
  • What happens before and after this step?
  • Who is involved in this process?
  • What information do you need to make a decision?
  • Where do delays, errors, or exceptions usually happen?
Output
Journey maps, workflow diagrams, process maps, pain point maps, opportunity areas, and requirements informed by real behaviour.
Why it matters
It helps the team design around the customer’s actual way of working, not only the system view or internal assumptions.
Feature discovery

Feature Discovery Interview

Understand the need, value, and context for a specific feature before designing it.

Purpose
To understand the need, value, and context for a specific feature before designing it.
When we use it
When a feature idea exists, but we need to better understand the problem it should solve and how it fits into the user’s workflow.
What we want to learn
Why the feature is needed, who needs it, what problem it solves, how often the problem occurs, and what outcome the user expects.
Typical questions
  • What problem would this feature help you solve?
  • When would you use it?
  • What information would you need to see?
  • What action would you expect to take from here?
  • What would make this feature valuable or not valuable to you?
Output
Feature goals, user needs, acceptance considerations, design principles, success criteria, and risks or assumptions.
Why it matters
It prevents the team from building a feature based only on internal assumptions or stakeholder requests.
Concept validation

Concept Feedback Session

Get early feedback on a design direction, concept, prototype, or proposed experience before it becomes final.

Purpose
To get early feedback on a design direction, concept, prototype, or proposed experience before it becomes final.
When we use it
When we have early wireframes, sketches, flows, or concept designs and need to understand whether the direction makes sense.
What we want to learn
Whether users understand the concept, whether it matches their expectations, what feels useful, what is confusing, and what may be missing.
Typical questions
  • What do you think this is showing you?
  • What would you do next from here?
  • What feels useful or not useful?
  • Is anything unclear?
  • How well does this fit into the way you work?
Output
Design feedback, comprehension issues, missing information, prioritised improvements, and confidence level in the proposed direction.
Why it matters
It helps us test the direction early, before too much time is invested in detailed design or development.
Usability

Usability Testing Session

Evaluate whether users can successfully use a design, prototype, or live feature.

Purpose
To evaluate whether users can successfully use a design, prototype, or live feature.
When we use it
When we have a prototype, clickable flow, or implemented feature that users can interact with.
What we want to learn
Whether users can complete key tasks, where they get stuck, what they misunderstand, and what needs to be improved.
Typical questions
  • Please show us how you would complete this task.
  • What would you click next?
  • What do you expect to happen here?
  • Was anything difficult or unclear?
  • How confident would you feel using this?
Output
Usability issues, severity ratings, task success observations, design recommendations, and changes needed before release.
Why it matters
It helps ensure that what we design is not only useful, but also understandable and usable.
Feedback

Customer Feedback Session

Gather structured feedback from customers on an existing product area, live feature, or recent change.

Purpose
To gather structured feedback from customers on an existing product area, live feature, or recent change.
When we use it
When we need feedback on something customers already use, or when we want to understand satisfaction, friction, and improvement areas.
What we want to learn
What works well, what does not, what causes friction, what customers value, and what they would improve.
Typical questions
  • How is this working for you today?
  • What do you use most often?
  • What feels difficult or inefficient?
  • What would you change?
  • What impact does this have on your day-to-day work?
Output
Feedback themes, improvement opportunities, product pain points, customer sentiment, and possible backlog items.
Why it matters
It gives the team a direct view of customer experience with the current product, rather than relying only on internal opinions or support tickets.
Innovation

Innovation / Future Opportunity Interview

Explore future-facing needs, unmet problems, and opportunities for product innovation.

Purpose
To explore future-facing needs, unmet problems, and opportunities for product innovation.
When we use it
When we are looking beyond immediate feature delivery and exploring where the product, workflow, or market could evolve.
What we want to learn
Emerging needs, strategic pain points, inefficient workflows, unmet expectations, future goals, and areas where the product could create new value.
Typical questions
  • What is changing in the way your team works?
  • What problems do you expect to become more important in the future?
  • What do you wish the product could help you with?
  • What takes too much time today?
  • If this process worked perfectly, what would that look like?
Output
Future opportunities, innovation themes, product vision input, strategic recommendations, and problem spaces for further discovery.
Why it matters
It helps the business think beyond incremental improvements and identify meaningful future value for customers.
Adoption

Post-release / Adoption Interview

Understand whether a released feature is being used, whether it delivers value, and what might be blocking adoption.

Purpose
To understand whether a released feature is being used, whether it delivers value, and what might be blocking adoption.
When we use it
After a feature has been released or piloted with customers.
What we want to learn
Whether users know the feature exists, whether they use it, how it fits into their workflow, what value it provides, and why adoption may be low or high.
Typical questions
  • Have you used this feature?
  • When do you use it?
  • What value does it provide?
  • What stops you from using it?
  • What would make it more useful?
Output
Adoption insights, value validation, improvement opportunities, usage barriers, and recommendations for iteration.
Why it matters
It helps us understand whether the feature is successful in real use, not just whether it was delivered.
Strategy

Strategic Customer Interview

Understand customer priorities, business goals, product expectations, and long-term relationship needs.

Purpose
To understand customer priorities, business goals, product expectations, and long-term relationship needs.
When we use it
When working with key customers, strategic accounts, or customers involved in roadmap conversations.
What we want to learn
Business priorities, strategic challenges, product expectations, perceived value, and areas where the product could better support their goals.
Typical questions
  • What are your main priorities this year?
  • Where does our product support or limit your goals?
  • What are the biggest operational challenges for your team?
  • What would make the product more valuable to your business?
  • What areas should we be thinking about longer term?
Output
Strategic insights, account-level themes, roadmap input, business needs, and opportunity areas.
Why it matters
It helps connect product and design decisions to customer business value, not only individual user needs.

Good practice

Principles for using customer interviews well

Use these as lightweight checks before planning, running, and documenting customer conversations.

01

Start with the decision

Customer interviews should be tied to a clear decision or learning goal.

02

Do not treat every request as a requirement

A single customer request does not automatically become a product requirement, but it may reveal an important underlying need.

03

Understand behaviour, not only opinion

What customers say is useful, but what they do, avoid, repeat, or work around is often more important.

04

Document impact

Research should show how findings influenced design, product decisions, or next steps.

05

Share evidence clearly

Use themes, quotes, examples, screenshots, recordings, or notes to help teams understand the insight.
